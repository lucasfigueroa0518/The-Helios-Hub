# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Tommy and the Helios team, all with the same access and no roles. They run an automated Instagram content machine: four content types (Trial Reels, Explainer Reels, Carousels, IG Stories) made overnight by separate pipelines and posted to one Instagram account. They open the Social Hub at a desk in the morning and on a phone during the day, usually with one of two questions: what is going out today and does anything need me, or what is working and what should we make more of.

## Product Purpose

The Social Hub is where the team operates and learns from the content machine. Operating comes first: today's produced content, what needs a person (approve, reject, regenerate, publish now, reschedule), and how full each type's idea pool and the shared music and photo libraries are. Learning comes second: how the account, each content type, and each post performed, with the cost to make it. Success is a morning check that takes a minute, an intervention that takes one click from wherever the content appears, and a performance question answered by drilling from the account to a type to a single post without losing your place.

## Positioning

One place to run the Helios content machine: see what's going out, step in only where a person is needed, and learn what's working.

## Brand Personality

Calm, exact, decisive. Quiet surfaces, confident numbers, one obvious next action on every screen. It should feel like a well-run control room, not a busy feed. The closest reference is Instagram's Professional Dashboard: familiar metric names the team already reads, and content-first thumbnails so a post is recognized by how it looks, not by an id.

## Anti-references

- Walls of equal tiles: dozens of same-weight stat boxes with no headline number and no comparison.
- A filter form first: a page that opens on controls before it shows an answer.
- Tab sprawl: many tabs of equal rank, so it's never clear where something lives.
- The generic SaaS template: big hero metrics, gradient accents, an uppercase eyebrow over every section, identical card grids.

## Design Principles

1. **Today first.** Every visit starts with what's going out today and what needs a person. Everything else is one step deeper.
2. **One question per screen.** Each page answers its main question at the top, in the order the eye reads; supporting detail follows, and the long tail is behind a click.
3. **Drill without losing your place.** The hierarchy is account, then content type, then post. Moving down keeps the list, the filters, and a way back; a post opens over the list it came from.
4. **Act where the content is.** Approve, reject, regenerate, publish now and reschedule sit on the content itself, wherever it appears, with the consequence stated before an expensive or irreversible click.
5. **Numbers earn their place.** A number appears with a comparison, a trend or a cost, or it doesn't appear.
