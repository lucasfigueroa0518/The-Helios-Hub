THE NEWS: Google updated a support page saying that from Oct 9, 2026 Gemini app users without a paid plan will be limited to Flash-Lite and AI Plus users lose Pro, while Gemini 4 Argon (announced Sept 30) is still in limited rollout; NPowerUser's claim that 3.6/3.7 Flash are being retired and 'Fast' modes pulled is unconfirmed. [F1, F2, F3, F6, F8]
WHY IT MATTERS:
- Free Gemini app users lose access to the Flash and Pro models on Oct 9, so the more capable models move behind paid plans; Google AI Pro subscribers gain Deep Think. [F1, F2, F4]
- Google's newest flagship, Gemini 4 Argon, is not yet widely available and will reach paid API customers and AI Ultra subscribers first. [F6, F7]
FACTS:
- F1: Starting Oct 9, Gemini app users without a Google AI subscription will be limited to the (3.5) Flash-Lite model and will no longer be able to access (3.6) Flash or (3.1) Pro. (9to5Google, PCMag, MakeUseOf)
    - Based on an updated Google support page; I could not open the support page itself. 9to5Google, PCMag and MakeUseOf all report it.
- F2: AI Plus subscribers will be limited to Flash-Lite and Flash and lose Pro, and will 'receive an email that explains when these changes will take effect.' (9to5Google, PCMag, MakeUseOf)
    - The AI Plus date is not fixed; per 9to5Google the email will say when the change applies to each account.
- F3: The Gemini app is also adding 'low,' 'medium,' and 'high' effort levels for each available model, which increase the model's ability to complete tasks but use more of the user's limit. (9to5Google, PCMag, MakeUseOf)
    - 9to5Google says the levels are expected in October and match what is in Google AI Studio and Antigravity; PCMag says users will 'soon' be able to choose.
- F4: AI Pro and AI Ultra see no regressions; AI Pro ($19.99/month) subscribers get Deep Think, currently only on the $99.99 and $199.99/month plans. (9to5Google)
    - Deep Think detail found only in 9to5Google among the pages I opened (Notebookcheck snippet from search also mentions it but I could not open that page).
- F5: Google's own Gemini API deprecations page (last updated 2026-10-01) lists gemini-3.6-flash and gemini-3.7-flash with 'No shutdown date announced', and gemini-3.8-flash as a September 2, 2026 release. (Google AI for Developers (Gemini deprecations page))
    - This does not support NPowerUser's claim that 3.6/3.7 Flash are being 'rapidly phased out'. The page was last updated Oct 1, before the NPowerUser article (Oct 5), so it may not reflect later changes.
- F6: Google announced Gemini 4 Argon on Sept 30, 2026, rolling it out first to trusted cyber defenders in its Fairwind Program, and says it will reach developers, enterprises and consumers 'as soon as possible', starting with paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers. (Google Blog, TechCrunch, Engadget, 9to5Google)
    - NPowerUser frames Argon as still 'nearing' and in 'grey-box testing'; the model has actually been formally announced with limited rollout.
- F7: 9to5Google says it is not clear where Argon will fit in the Gemini app, and Google has not specified whether Argon is a Pro model or a new tier above it. (9to5Google)
- F8: [CLAIM: NPowerUser says] [CLAIM: NPowerUser says] Google is retiring Gemini 3.6 Flash and 3.7 Flash in favor of Gemini 3.8 Flash and has pulled dedicated 'Fast' modes from model selectors in favor of auto-routing, with Flash-Lite handling low-latency tasks. (NPowerUser)
    - Not confirmed by any independent source or by Google's deprecations page (F5). The article cites no Google announcement and gives no shutdown dates. It also contains internal oddities (e.g. a table citing a 3.5/3.6 Flash retirement, a 'Gemini Flash checkpoint' reference). Treat as unverified.
- F9: Argon is priced at an introductory $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens and has a 1M-token output limit, up from 64K. (Google Blog, Engadget)
    - Google's figures; Engadget cites Artificial Analysis comparing cost with GPT-6 Astra.

TERMS:
- Flash-Lite / Flash / Pro: Gemini's three model tiers: Flash-Lite is the lightest and fastest, Flash the all-around middle option, Pro the most advanced. (PCMag)
- Effort levels (low/medium/high): A setting that raises a model's ability to complete tasks and give more thorough answers, but uses more of the user's usage limit. (9to5Google / PCMag)
- Deep Think: A Gemini option for 'maximum parallel reasoning', currently on the top-tier Ultra plans and coming to AI Pro. (9to5Google)
- Fairwind Program: Google's security initiative for governments and trusted partners needing models with the most advanced cybersecurity capabilities; first recipients of Gemini 4 Argon. (TechCrunch / Engadget)
- Deprecation vs. shutdown: Deprecation is the announcement that Google no longer supports a model and it will be shut down soon; shutdown means the endpoint is completely turned off. (Google AI for Developers (Gemini deprecations page))
