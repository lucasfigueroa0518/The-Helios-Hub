THE NEWS: Google has updated a Gemini support page to say that from October 9, 2026, free Gemini users keep only the Flash-Lite model and AI Plus subscribers lose the Pro model; AI Pro keeps all three models and gains Deep Think (per Google's page, as reported by PCMag, MakeUseOf and The Mac Observer). [F1, F2, F3, F4]
WHY IT MATTERS:
- Free users lose the middle and top Gemini models, so the strongest models now sit behind paid plans from $4.99 (Flash) and $19.99 (Pro) a month. This is a clear nudge to upgrade. [F1, F2, F3, N2]
- Google is not alone. MakeUseOf reports OpenAI has cut advanced-model allowances on some paid plans, and ties the trend to the high cost of running top models. PCMag points to rising storage and component costs. [F8, F9]
FACTS:
- F1: Starting October 9, people using Gemini without a paid Google AI plan will only be able to use the Flash-Lite model, and will lose the Flash and Pro models they could use until now. (PCMag, MakeUseOf, The Mac Observer)
    - Outlets say this comes from an updated Google support page, which I did not open directly. PCMag credits 9to5Google for spotting the change; I did not open 9to5Google. PCMag says free users have had limited access to all three models so far.
- F2: AI Plus subscribers ($4.99 per month) will keep Flash-Lite and Flash but lose Pro, and Google will email them about when the change takes effect for their account. (PCMag, MakeUseOf, The Mac Observer)
- F3: AI Pro ($19.99 per month) keeps Flash-Lite, Flash and Pro, and gains Deep Think, which is currently only on the $99.99 and $199.99 per month plans. (The Mac Observer, PCMag, MakeUseOf)
    - The Deep Think addition and the $99.99 and $199.99 prices are only in The Mac Observer. MakeUseOf says Pro is available on AI Pro and AI Ultra. PCMag says Pro unlocks with AI Pro.
- F4: Gemini will also add Low, Medium and High effort levels for its models. Higher effort helps the model finish tasks and answer more thoroughly, but uses up the user's limit faster. (PCMag, MakeUseOf, The Mac Observer)
    - PCMag quotes Google's support page; The Mac Observer says the effort levels arrive 'in October'.
- F5: PCMag gives specific model versions: free users lose 3.6 Flash and 3.1 Pro and keep only 3.5 Flash-Lite. AI Plus unlocks 3.6 Flash, and AI Pro unlocks 3.1 Pro. (PCMag)
    - The other outlets name the models without version numbers.
- F6: AI Plus and AI Pro plans offer 2x and 5x Gemini usage limits compared with the free plan. Google doesn't explain how it calculates these limits, saying only that usage factors in prompt complexity, features used and chat length. (PCMag)
- F7: Students remain eligible for a year of Google's AI Pro plan for free, and this is the second time Google has overhauled its AI plans this year. (PCMag)
- F8: MakeUseOf reports OpenAI recently halved advanced-model allowances on some paid plans and gave some existing Pro users free GPT credits to soften the blow. It says Anthropic went the other way, raising five-hour usage limits by 20 percent with the launch of Claude Opus 5.5. (MakeUseOf)
    - Single-source for the OpenAI and Anthropic details. They come from MakeUseOf's reporting, with no outside confirmation.
- F9: PCMag links the timing to rising storage and component costs that could affect Google's AI infrastructure spending. The Mac Observer says Google is making the changes to manage AI resources, and that the Gemini 4 Argon model is expected to arrive first for AI Ultra subscribers. (PCMag, The Mac Observer)
    - These are the outlets' own interpretations, hedged with words like 'potentially' and 'likely'. Google's stated reason was not found in the sources.
BACKGROUND:
- B1: MakeUseOf says running top AI models is expensive and that many AI companies still spend far more than they earn. It cites reports that OpenAI made $13.07 billion in revenue in 2025 and posted a $38.5 billion net loss. (MakeUseOf)
    - The OpenAI figure is described as 'reportedly'. It is not independently verified here.
