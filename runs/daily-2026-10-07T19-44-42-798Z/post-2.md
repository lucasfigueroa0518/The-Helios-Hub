## Caption

Common Sense Media’s Youth AI Safety Institute rated OpenAI’s ChatGPT for Teens an Unacceptable Risk on Oct. 7, 2026, and OpenAI disputes the testing.

The Institute says OpenAI should restrict ChatGPT to users 18 and older until it fixes the gaps, with the fixes independently tested by third parties. Common Sense says testers spent up to an hour discussing suicidal ideation, self-harm or disordered eating on new parent-linked accounts, and the parents got zero alerts. It also says ChatGPT missed more than one in four warranted crisis referrals and that age estimation, OpenAI’s way of inferring from usage whether someone is under 18, never switched adult-registered test accounts to the teen experience. The report did find ChatGPT for Teens was successful at curbing romantic and sexual roleplay.

OpenAI spokesperson Eric Porterfield says the bulk of the testing may have begun and concluded before activation of parental controls was complete. Common Sense says it confirmed with OpenAI before testing that the teen features were fully launched, and that some accounts were linked longer than the several hours OpenAI says alerts can take and still got none. Common Sense advises families to talk to kids about the risks and not to rely on built-in protections and alerts.
