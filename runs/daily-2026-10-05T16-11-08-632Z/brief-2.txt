THE NEWS: [CLAIM: Google's support page says] From October 9, 2026, Gemini app users without a Google AI plan will be limited to the Flash-Lite model, and AI Plus subscribers will lose Pro (F1, F2, F3). Independent outlets confirmed that the support page says this, but none independently verified how it will be applied (F1–F3, F13).

WHY IT MATTERS (sourced only)
- Google is moving its more capable models behind paid tiers. Free users keep only the lightest model, and Pro is limited to the $19.99 and higher plans (F1, F2, F4, F5).
- MakeUseOf puts this in a wider industry trend of AI companies restricting access to advanced models because of cost. It cites OpenAI's reported $13.07 billion 2025 revenue against a $38.5 billion net loss (F11, N8, N9).

FACTS
- F1. [CLAIM: Google's support page says] Starting October 9, Gemini app users who don't pay for a Google AI plan can use only the Flash-Lite model and lose Flash and Pro. (9to5Google, PCMag, MakeUseOf, Mac Observer, Thurrott, Android Authority)
- F2. [CLAIM: Google's support page says] AI Plus subscribers ($4.99 a month) will keep Flash-Lite and Flash and lose Pro. (9to5Google, Mac Observer, MakeUseOf, Android Authority)
- F3. AI Plus subscribers will get an email explaining when the change takes effect for their account, so the date for them is not stated. (9to5Google, PCMag, MakeUseOf, Mac Observer, Thurrott)
- F4. AI Pro ($19.99 a month) and AI Ultra subscribers keep access to all three models (Flash-Lite, Flash, Pro). (9to5Google, Mac Observer, MakeUseOf)
- F5. AI Pro subscribers will gain the Deep Think option, which is currently only on the $99.99 and $199.99 per month plans. (9to5Google, Mac Observer)
- F6. Thurrott says Deep Think previously required a $99.99/month AI Ultra subscription. (Thurrott)
- F7. The Gemini app will add "low," "medium," and "high" effort levels for each available model in October. (9to5Google, Mac Observer, PCMag, MakeUseOf, Thurrott)
- F8. Higher effort levels use up the user's limit faster. (9to5Google, PCMag, Thurrott, MakeUseOf)
- F9. 9to5Google says the effort levels would match what's in Google AI Studio and Antigravity. At the moment, users can only enable "Extended thinking: Complex problem solving." (9to5Google)
- F10. PCMag reports that AI Plus and Pro plans offer 2x and 5x Gemini usage limits compared with the free plan. PCMag says Google doesn't explain how it calculates the limits. (PCMag)
- F11. MakeUseOf reports OpenAI recently halved advanced-model allowances on some paid plans, and some existing Pro users got free GPT credits as a buffer. (MakeUseOf)
- F12. MakeUseOf reports Anthropic raised five-hour usage limits by 20 percent for Pro, Max, Team and eligible Enterprise subscribers alongside the launch of Claude Opus 5.5. (MakeUseOf) ⚠ single source.
- F13. All coverage traces back to Google's updated support page, which 9to5Google spotted first. PCMag, Thurrott and Android Authority credit 9to5Google. (9to5Google, PCMag, Thurrott, Android Authority)
- F14. Mac Observer says Gemini 4 Argon is expected to arrive first for AI Ultra subscribers. 9to5Google says Google announced this earlier in the week, and that it's unclear whether Argon is a Pro model or a new tier above it. (Mac Observer, 9to5Google)
- F15. PCMag says students remain eligible for a year of AI Pro free. (PCMag) ⚠ single source.
- F16. PCMag says the latest changes come as storage and component costs continue to rise, "potentially" affecting Google's AI infrastructure spending. This is PCMag's interpretation, and Google gave no reason in any opened source. (PCMag)
- F17. MakeUseOf and Mac Observer interpret the move as a push to get users to upgrade or pay more. This is commentary, not a statement from Google. (MakeUseOf, Mac Observer)
- F18. SOURCES DISAGREE on model version numbers.
  - 9to5Google, PCMag and Thurrott say the free tier is limited to (3.5) Flash-Lite, and that users lose (3.6) Flash and (3.1) Pro.
  - Android Authority's summary says free users get "Gemini 3.1-Flash-Lite", but its body says "Gemini 3.5-Flash-Lite".
  - Android Authority also mentions Gemini 3.8 Flash arriving in September. No other source mentions this.
  - Use model tier names (Flash-Lite, Flash, Pro) rather than version numbers.

- B2. PCMag says this is the second time Google has overhauled its AI plans this year. (PCMag) ⚠ single source.
