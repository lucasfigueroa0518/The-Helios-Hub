# 07 — Sound design for emotion (the sound designer's brain)

Desk research, 2026-10-29. Scope: music cues, stingers, tension and ambience for 60–75 s vertical
mini-documentaries, planned by an LLM sound-designer step after picture and narration timing are
locked. Written in answer to the owner's critique: music and sound "weren't used enough" to move the
viewer, and the cold open had no audio tension.

Complements, and does not repeat: 01 §7 (score as tension engine, ethics of sound) and 03 §10–12
(music and tension, foley, mix and loudness). Read those for drones/ostinato, act changes, foley
literalism, ducking depth and the −14 LUFS target.

**How to read a line:** `Do X — because Y. [S#] (evidence | lore | proposal)`
- **evidence** = study, standard or platform documentation ("platform-run" flagged).
- **lore** = practitioner craft, unmeasured. **proposal** = a default derived here; tune by ear.

## 0. Correction to the brief: the "80% muted" premise

The 80%-muted figure is not supported for TikTok or Reels. TikTok's own research says 88% of users
call sound vital to the experience (platform-run). Secondary sources put Reels sound-on at about
60% to over 80%. The often-quoted "75% of mobile views are silent" (HubSpot) covers older feed
video on Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn, not Reels or TikTok. [S1][S2][S3]
**Implication:** keep designing picture to work muted (03 §2, §8), but assume most viewers hear the
reel. Sound is a main channel, not a bonus for a minority.

## 1. Cold open: tension from frame 0

- **Start audio on frame 0 under the first spoken word, with no fade-in** — because the first second decides the swipe and a fade-in sounds like nothing is happening (see 03 §2). [S4] (lore)
- **Open on a tension bed (low drone, pulse or ticking figure), not a full music track** — because a low, evolving bed builds the unease that the later payoff needs. [S5] (lore)
- **Shape the cold open as one arc: bed → riser → near-silence → hit, with the hit on the hook's reveal word or title card** — because tension then release is the core trailer move, and short-form cold opens are trailers. [S5][S6][S7] (lore)
- **Cut to near-silence for 0.3–1.0 s right before the biggest hit** — because after a drop the hit sounds much louder without being louder. [S5] (lore)
- **End the riser exactly on the hit frame by starting it one riser-length earlier** — because a riser that peaks early or late reads as a mistake; risers almost always resolve into an impact, sub-drop or downbeat. [S8] (lore)
- **For sustained dread across a passage, use an endlessly rising figure (Shepard-tone style) instead of repeated hits** — because the illusion of constant rise keeps tension without fatigue, as in Zimmer's *Dunkirk* score. [S9] (lore)

## 2. Music editing

- **Pick music by act, not by reel: a tension bed for the setup, a shift at the turn, a release or resolve for the close** — because changing or restarting music marks each act for the ear (see 03 §10). [S10][S11] (lore)
- **Switch music on the turn word, not on the cut before it** — because the music change should confirm the story change the voice just made. [S11] (proposal)
- **Hard-stop the music on a reveal, let the line land in near-silence, then re-enter on a hit or a new cue** — because a sudden drop-out is the strongest emphasis an editor has, and big moments land harder against restraint. [S12][S13] (lore)
- **Edit music only at bar or phrase boundaries, except a deliberate hard stop on a hit** — because a cut mid-phrase sounds like an error to most listeners. [S14] (lore)
- **Swell into an emotional beat over 2–4 s, peak on it, then pull back** — because a crescendo prepares the feeling instead of announcing it after the fact. [S11] (lore)
- **End on a button (a final hit or chord on the last frame), never a fade** — because a fade reads as "the video ran out", and a clean button helps a loop restart. [S6] (lore)
- **Don't put a hit on every cut; reserve synced hits for turns and reveals** — because a cut-per-beat grid feels mechanical and spends the effect (see 03 §10). [S12][S13] (lore)
- **Choose empathetic music (matching the feeling) by default, and anempathetic music (calm against chaos) only for a deliberate irony beat** — because Chion shows music can heighten a scene by agreeing with it or by its indifference. [S15] (lore)
- **Check that removing the music would not change the facts the viewer takes away** — because music shapes how factual claims are read, and the score must not take sides (see 01 §7). [S16] (lore)

## 3. Spot effects, stingers and transitions

- **Sync a sound to the frame where a visual element arrives or changes** — because sound and image struck together fuse into one event and gain weight (synchresis, "added value"). [S15] (lore, widely accepted theory)
- **Give every whoosh a visible cause, and match its length to the motion** — because an unmotivated whoosh reads as a template (see 03 anti-patterns). [S6] (lore)
- **Lead scene changes with the next scene's sound 0.2–1 s before the picture (a sound bridge)** — because it pulls the viewer across the cut. [S12] (lore)
- **Use one short recurring stinger motif for the series at the same structural spot (e.g. the title or the "what happened next" turn)** — because a repeated sonic signature builds recognition across episodes. [S17] (lore)
- **Build impacts from layers (transient click, body, low tail), but allow only one big impact per ~10–15 s** — because dynamics are what make impacts land; constant impacts flatten the mix. [S5][S7] (lore)
- **Never let two big hits fall within about 2 s of each other** — because the second masks the first and neither registers. [S5] (proposal)

## 4. Ambience and silence

- **Never use digital silence; keep room tone or a faint air bed under every "silent" moment** — because true zero sounds like a dropout, while quiet room tone still reads as a place. [S18] (lore)
- **Give each setting its own low ambience (office hum, crowd murmur, rain, keyboard clatter)** — because place sound makes a still into a scene before music does (see 01 §7). [S18][S19] (lore)
- **Keep no more than two or three distinct layers audible at once (voice, one music layer, one effects/ambience layer)** — because Murch found listeners can follow about two and a half layers of the same kind before clarity collapses. [S20] (lore)

## 5. Mix and platform delivery

- **Let the music duck under the voice with a slow release so it swells back in the pauses** — because a breathing duck keeps emotion moving without fighting the words (depth: see 03 §12). [S21] (lore)
- **Put a mid-frequency layer (roughly 200 Hz–2 kHz) on every sub-drop and low boom** — because phone speakers barely reproduce sub-bass, so a pure sub hit is silent on most devices. [S22] (lore)
- **Master to about −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP, and keep dynamic range** — because platforms turn loud uploads down, so over-compression only flattens the impacts (TikTok and Meta publish no official target). [S23][S24] (lore)
- **For muted viewers, give each big sound a visual twin (a caption emphasis, a flash, a frame shake, a stamp)** — because the same moment then lands with or without sound. [S15][S25] (proposal)

## 6. Cue vocabulary for the sound-designer LLM

Every cue is anchored to a locked time: `word:<id>` (the onset of a narration word), `cut:<frame#>`,
or `t:<seconds>`. Each cue carries an `intent` (one line: why this cue, what feeling).

| Cue | Parameters | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| `bed.start` | mood (tension, wonder, dread, drive, grief, resolve), intensity 1–3 | opening a music section |
| `bed.switch` | new mood | act turn, on the turn word |
| `bed.swell` | from, to (2–4 s) | approach to an emotional beat |
| `bed.drop` | hard-stop at anchor | reveal; leaves voice alone |
| `bed.button` | final hit on last frame | ending / loop point |
| `drone` | pitch (low/mid), length | tension under the cold open or a dread passage |
| `riser` | length (1–6 s), `lands_on` anchor | build into a hit; ends exactly on the anchor |
| `silence` | 0.3–1.0 s, room tone kept | the gap before the biggest hit |
| `impact` | weight (light/heavy), with sub + mid layer | reveal, title, turn |
| `sub_drop` | always with a mid layer | release after a riser or a heavy fact |
| `stinger` | series motif | title card, fixed structural spot |
| `whoosh` | length matched to the motion | a visible element or camera move |
| `reverse_swell` | 0.5–1.5 s into the cut | sucks into a hard cut or reveal |
| `pulse` | ticking clock, heartbeat, tempo | deadline, pressure, countdown |
| `foley` | paper, pen, stamp, shutter, typing, pin | key visual events (03 §11) |
| `ambience` | place, level | each new setting |
| `bridge` | lead time 0.2–1 s | the next scene's sound before its picture |

## 7. Density guideline per 60 s (proposal; tune by ear)

| Cue family | Count per 60 s |
|---|---|
| Music sections (`bed.start`/`bed.switch`) | 2–3 (setup, turn, close) |
| `bed.drop` | 1–2 (the main reveal, maybe the turn) |
| `riser` → `impact`/`sub_drop` pairs | 2–4, one in the cold open |
| `silence` | 1–2, before the biggest hits |
| `stinger` | 1 |
| `whoosh` / `reverse_swell` | 4–8, motivated only |
| `foley` accents | 6–12 |
| `ambience` changes | 1 per new setting (typically 2–4) |
| **All discrete spot cues** | **about 15–25** |

Hard limits: at most one heavy impact per ~10–15 s; no two big hits within ~2 s; no more than three
layers audible at once; the cold open (first ~3–6 s) always has a bed and one riser → hit arc.

## Anti-patterns (don't)

- Don't open on music with no tension shape, or fade in from silence.
- Don't run one track unchanged from first frame to last.
- Don't put a hit on every cut, or stack impacts back to back.
- Don't fade out at the end; button it.
- Don't use whooshes, glitches or risers with nothing on screen to cause them.
- Don't let any gap fall to digital silence.
- Don't rely on sub-bass alone; phones won't play it.
- Don't let music tell the viewer which side of a dispute to take.
