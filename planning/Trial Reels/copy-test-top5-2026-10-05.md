Caption:

> Men in AI jobs typically earn $45,000 more than women, and women are likelier to hold the jobs AI could replace.
>
> The Guardian reported on new LinkedIn research. AI job postings have doubled since 2023, and those jobs pay on average more than twice as much as roles that don't involve AI. Women made up only about a quarter of new hires in AI roles in the last year, compared to 50% of new hires in non-AI roles. In executive AI roles, the share drops to 13%.
>
> One caveat from the report: the $45,000 gap is the difference in median pay across all AI occupations, and it is partly due to the types of jobs men are likelier to have. Women who do have AI jobs are disproportionately in low-paying roles like data annotators, according to LinkedIn's Sarah Steinberg. So part of the gap comes from which jobs women get hired into.
>
> The other side of the number: LinkedIn's research also shows women are more likely to work in roles with high exposure to AI disruption, like customer service. That puts them at higher risk of losing work to AI.
>
> Why it happens, according to the people quoted. Brenda Darden Wilkerson of AnitaB.org says companies are hiring at breakneck speed through the same networks, referrals and filters they always used, which hasn't given women the same exposure. Companies have also scaled back their diversity programs. Jayeeta Putatunda, an AI engineering lead, came back from four months of maternity leave to find completely different frameworks and models. In her words, women may lack the infrastructure that makes keeping up possible, not the ability.
>
> Felicia Newhouse of AI Powered Women frames what is at stake as who captures a major new source of economic mobility.
