Caption:

> The rules meant to keep the AI you use in check were reportedly written with help from a man who runs an AI company. Then he signed them.
>
> That's the story behind a deal Trump and six AI CEOs signed last Tuesday, according to The Guardian's TechScape newsletter.
>
> The goal, on paper: put controls on AI. Trump called the deal "morally binding." Elon Musk, Mark Zuckerberg, Jensen Huang and Dario Amodei were among the signers.
>
> Who wrote it? Zuckerberg hand-guided the writing, reportedly. The idea came out of a conversation between him and House speaker Mike Johnson at the state dinner for Xi Jinping. Zuckerberg sent around a draft before the CEOs met, and Huang backed it.
>
> What's in it? Not much, by The Guardian's account. The deal is vague. It sets up what Trump called "tremendous self-policing," which means AI companies act as their own watchmen.
>
> Trump compared it to a constitution: "It's almost like a constitution in a way." The Guardian's reply: constitutions are legally binding, and this agreement is not.
>
> So for the AI you use, the watchdog named in the deal is the company that makes it. The promise has no legal force behind it.
>
> There's a money side too. The Guardian says the companies whose leaders came to the summit drove most of the US stock market's growth over the past five years. The people writing the rules are the same people the economy now leans on.
