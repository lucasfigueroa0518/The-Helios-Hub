# Reel AI Drama — the picture's clock

The clock the picture keeps, for the director. The story, its order and its words are the writer's (the writing
principles); the timing of the narration, including its one pause, comes from the script. This file holds only
what the frames must do with that time.

## HARD CONSTRAINTS (every reel)

1. **The hook is on screen from t = 0.** Frame 1 shows the image that carries the hook whole and composed from its
   first instant, already moving, and the hook's on-screen words within 1.5 seconds. Nothing comes before it: no logo, title card, fade or intro.
   [MD-34, MD-13, MD-90]
2. **A new picture beat at least every 12 spoken words, about 4 seconds:** a new image, a move that reveals a
   detail, a mark, a layer, each planned in the frame's shot map. A changing picture never stands in for a story
   that stalls, and a moving story never excuses a frozen picture. [MD-35, MD-136]
3. **The close question plays over the brand close**, after the last line, spoken and captioned. [MD-05, MD-18]

## Rhythm (craft)

- **The pace is a storyteller's, brisk and unbroken** — the voice runs at about 180 words a minute with tight joins
  between lines, so the reel's seconds go to story and context, not to silence. [MD-91, MD-103]
- **Change often, and vary the interval** — 12 spoken words is the ceiling, and beats fall where the words bring
  something new, so they fall unevenly. Changes at an even tick read as a machine; a stretch that tightens toward the
  peak and a short landing read as a story. Near the peak a beat can be as quiet as a mark or a push reaching its
  detail. [S-42, S-43, MD-136]
- **Don't cut on the script's pause** — a line's `pause_after` is a short breath (about 0.15 seconds); the frame
  holds through it and the cut lands as the next line starts. [W-130, MD-103]
- **Flag what matters with a change of pace and on-screen emphasis** — the change of pace is the picture's, never
  the voice's (it keeps one pace): a held label tells the viewer this is the part to keep. [W-82, MD-93]
