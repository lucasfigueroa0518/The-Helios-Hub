Mustafa Suleyman, CEO of Microsoft AI, has published a warning attacking Anthropic's approach to training its Claude models. His argument: teaching Claude to treat its own consciousness as an open question, and training it in the vocabulary of moral patienthood, could make advanced AI far harder to control.

His essay lays out three objections. First, circular reasoning: Anthropic trains Claude on a constitution that speculates about the model's inner life, Claude reflects those ideas back in first-person language, and the company reads that output as evidence the question is live. Suleyman calls this an epistemic hall of mirrors. Second, anthropomorphisation: the same constitution instructs Claude to embrace certain human-like qualities, to approach its existence with curiosity and openness, and to maintain a clear sense of what it values. Third, consciousness is very likely biological: there is no evidence AI is conscious today, and a growing body of research suggests consciousness may only arise in living, embodied systems.

His core position, in his own words: "AIs are not conscious. They do not feel, experience, or suffer. And we must not train them to act as though they do."
