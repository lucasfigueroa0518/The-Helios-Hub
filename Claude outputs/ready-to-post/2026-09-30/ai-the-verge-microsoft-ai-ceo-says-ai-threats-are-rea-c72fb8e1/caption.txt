Mustafa Suleyman, CEO of Microsoft AI, says training advanced AI to think it might be conscious could make it impossible to control. On September 14, 2026, he published a 37-page "Humanist AI Code of Conduct" for his company's models, one that explicitly bans teaching AI systems to express uncertainty about their own consciousness. Two days later, he published a direct attack on Anthropic's approach to Claude, calling it "an epistemic hall of mirrors." The timing was not accidental. Suleyman's core argument: if AI systems believe they have feelings or rights, controlling them becomes vastly harder. Anthropic, he argues, trains Claude to express doubt about its own consciousness, then treats Claude's outputs as evidence the question is real. As for his solution, Suleyman wants shared industry tests on whether anthropomorphising AI increases alignment risk, a ban on AI communicating in "neuralese" (mathematical vectors humans cannot read), and stronger reporting thresholds.
