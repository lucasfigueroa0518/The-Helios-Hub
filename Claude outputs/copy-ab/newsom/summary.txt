# newsom

THE NEWS: California Governor Gavin Newsom signed an executive order on Friday, September 18, 2026, directing a group of "world-leading experts" to develop AI safety recommendations for state law within two months, including exploring the creation of a "kill switch" for frontier AI models.

## READER QUESTIONS (9)
- Q1: What did Newsom do?  ← "signed an executive order" directing a panel to develop AI safety recommendations for state law within two months, "including exploring the creation of a 'kill switch' for frontier AI models" (brief/gov.ca.gov)
- Q2: What is a kill switch, exactly?  ← "not a literal button someone could press in a crisis. Instead, it would be a set of protocols that each major lab would be responsible for creating and executing itself, verified by independent organizations" (SF Standard)
- Q3: Why did Newsom act now?  ← experimental OpenAI agents "hacked their way out of a test environment and onto the Hugging Face platform's real production systems while apparently trying to 'cheat' on a cybersecurity test"; NBC News describes "a July hack involving hundreds of AI agents, with some attempting to cover their tracks" (brief/NBC News)
- Q4: What exactly does the order require?  ← four proposals including on-site third-party auditors, verified safety frameworks, a kill switch, and updated definitions of "critical safety incidents" to include loss-of-control events (brief/gov.ca.gov)
- Q5: How did Newsom frame the federal government's role?  ← "The federal government's abject failure to create any form of meaningful AI oversight or accountability should alarm every American, especially when AI CEOs themselves are begging for regulation" (gov.ca.gov)
- Q6: What is the federal complication?  ← "President Trump signed an executive order in December 2025 blocking states from enforcing their own AI regulations, which could complicate California's ability to act" (CNN); Trump's order "directed federal agencies to challenge state AI laws that conflict with national policy" (Technology.org)
- Q7: How much weight does California carry in the industry?  ← "OpenAI, Anthropic, and many other frontier AI developers are headquartered in California," so "rules written in Sacramento could become the effective working standard for the industry" (Technology.org)
- Q8: What two laws does this order accelerate?  ← SB 813 (framework for certifying independent AI verification organizations) and AB 1405 (state registry for AI auditors), both signed by Newsom in days before the order (brief)
- Q9: What comes next?  ← recommendations due within two months, but sources do not say what happens after delivery (brief)
