California Gov. Gavin Newsom signed an executive order on September 18 directing a panel of AI experts to recommend new state safety rules within two months, including a possible kill switch: an emergency shutoff for the most advanced AI models.

The panel is asked to consider four proposals: embedding independent auditors inside AI labs, requiring outside verification of company safety reports, advancing the kill switch, and updating what counts as a critical safety incident. A kill switch, experts say, would not be a literal button anyone could press. It would be a set of protocols each major lab creates and runs itself, verified by an independent organization.

The order is limited in reach — California can only regulate so much of an industry that operates across state lines — but its decisions still carry weight: as the SF Standard puts it, California "sets trends that often end up determining how things get done at the federal level."
