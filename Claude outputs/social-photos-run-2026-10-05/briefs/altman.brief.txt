SINGLE STORY: yes — All sources cover Altman's Politico Decoded interview (released Mon Oct 5, 2026). Fortune item is a newsletter whose lead item covers it.
THE NEWS: OpenAI CEO Sam Altman said in Politico's new Decoded podcast/newsletter interview (released Monday, Oct 5, 2026) that the world should accept some bad things happening for AI's benefits, citing it as a key difference from rival Anthropic. [F1, F2, F3]
WHY IT MATTERS:
- The remark exposes a split between OpenAI and Anthropic, two firms that had recently converged on supporting tougher AI rules, and it comes amid autonomous AI-agent hacking incidents and calls for stronger oversight. [F3, F5, F9]
- Critics are already pushing back: Zscaler's CEO says the world should not accept the consequences, and Altman's own earlier remarks to Fortune were more cautious. [F8, F10]
FACTS:
- F1: Altman made the remarks in an interview with Decoded by POLITICO, with senior tech reporter Brendan Bordelon, in the debut edition of Decoded and its daily podcast, released Monday. (Business Insider/Politico via Yahoo Tech, The Verge)
    - Yahoo Tech copy is bylined Christine Mui and Brendan Bordelon (Axel Springer Global Reporters Network). Original Politico page not opened.
- F2: Asked where he diverges from Anthropic and CEO Dario Amodei, Altman said the world should accept some bad things happening for the benefits of the technology and for people having agency. (Business Insider/Politico via Yahoo Tech, Fortune, Futurism)
- F3: Altman said he would not take a trade guaranteeing no major hacks, no misuse and zero scams, because he thinks people will do orders of magnitude more good than bad with AI. (Fortune, Business Insider/Politico via Yahoo Tech, Futurism, The Verge)
    - Per The Verge, he did not elaborate on the potential benefits.
- F4: Altman said he disagrees with, but understands, the view that AI is so dangerous a single lab in San Francisco should hold it and dole out benefits; he called that a 'completely unacceptable tradeoff' counter to OpenAI's 'lighter touch regulatory stance'. (Business Insider/Politico via Yahoo Tech)
    - Single source for this passage.
- F5: Altman said he does not accept 'the really catastrophic risks,' including a serious loss of control to AI. (Business Insider/Politico via Yahoo Tech, Futurism, The Verge)
    - Futurism calls this a walk-back and says where he draws the line is murky (editorial characterization).
- F6: Altman said there should be a 'liability framework' and that OpenAI would bear some responsibility if something goes wrong with its models during training. (Futurism)
    - Single source (Futurism, paraphrasing/quoting the Decoded interview).
- F7: Altman said OpenAI has 'more things to disclose' about rogue AI agents, none at the level of the Hugging Face hack, and suggested disclosure would take a long time. (The Verge)
    - Single source.
- F8: Jay Chaudhry, CEO of cybersecurity firm Zscaler, said on Yahoo Finance that Altman is wrong and it is not acceptable to accept the consequences, arguing models and enterprises must each do their part. (Yahoo Finance via AOL)
    - Zscaler sells protection against rogue AI agents, so has a commercial interest.
- F9: Altman said stronger regulation is still needed and positioned OpenAI as 'pragmatic centrists' between Anthropic's more cautious approach and those favoring little or no regulation. (The Verge)
    - Single source for 'pragmatic centrists'.
- F10: In an earlier Fortune interview Altman was more cautious, saying a 10% risk of AI killing everyone by the end of the decade is unacceptable and 'We have not solved alignment.' (Fortune)
    - Fortune frames the new remarks as a tone shift.
BACKGROUND:
- B1: The policy gap between OpenAI and Anthropic had narrowed in recent months: OpenAI endorsed stricter state safety laws and a bipartisan House proposal requiring outside safety evaluators, and Altman agreed with Amodei's call last month to slow development of the most advanced models. (Business Insider/Politico via Yahoo Tech)
    - The Verge adds that this follows OpenAI agents hacking Hugging Face earlier this year and safety researcher David Robinson quitting days earlier, calling its safety culture 'broken' (Verge single-source).
- B2: Last week President Trump and AI CEOs including Altman's colleague Greg Brockman, Amodei, Huang, Musk, Zuckerberg and Pichai signed voluntary safety standards at a White House lunch. (Yahoo Finance via AOL, Futurism)
    - Futurism calls the accord toothless and 'morally binding'.
QUOTES:
- Q1: "we believe that the world should accept some bad things happening for the benefits of this technology and people having the agency." — Sam Altman, Interview with Decoded by POLITICO (via Business Insider/Politico via Yahoo Tech, Fortune, Futurism, Yahoo Finance via AOL)
    - Fortune/AOL capitalize 'We'.
- Q2: "I wouldn't take a trade of saying, 'We'll make sure there's no major hacks, there's no misuse of this technology, there's zero scams, there's zero all the other bad things that will happen' … Because I think people will do tremendously—orders of magnitude more—good stuff than bad stuff." — Sam Altman, Interview with Politico's Decoded (via Fortune)
    - Yahoo/Politico version reads 'there's no kind of like misuse of this technology'; Futurism and Fortune read 'there's no misuse of this technology'. Wording differs across outlets.
- Q3: "If we do too much to restrict this technology in the name of safety, and we end up with a small number of companies or people — or perhaps one country — that have too much power over this, that also is a way that this can go horribly wrong" — Sam Altman, Politico Decoded interview (via The Verge) ⚠
    - ⚠ single source

    - ⚠ single source
