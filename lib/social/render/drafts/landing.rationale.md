Composition: one word dwarfs the line. The first hook span of the headline is set at about 2.2x the surrounding words, sized to the measure, and stands with its baseline flush on a ground that runs edge to edge: a slim 1080x264 scene strip when there is a photo, a single hairline when there is not.
The type is the slide. Above the ground: the lead words. Below it: a long pause of empty canvas, then a small body line and a quiet note, bottom left, with nothing in between.
Eye path: Entry = the orange verb (y about 43%, inside the grid crop). Read = the lead words stacked just above it. Settle = body and note after the black pause, bottom left, clear of the credit pill.
The one orange moment: the verb. A second hook in the headline or body is kept as weight, not colour, so the accent is never doubled.
Why it beats a title-over-photo: the photo is not a backdrop or a card, it is the floor the sentence lands on, so scale, edge and silence do the work. Nothing sits on the photo, so no scrim is needed and no face is ever under type.
Canvases: black, white, green and orange verified; on orange the verb becomes the black hook chip, tightened to the word.
