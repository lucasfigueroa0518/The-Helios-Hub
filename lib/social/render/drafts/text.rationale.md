Composition: a hard scale cliff. The headline is set monumental (up to ~150px in photo modes, ~200px type-only) with its last line pushed to the right margin, so a short headline reads as a Z. The body drops to a small column hung on the page axis (x = 540, the canvas centre) under the headline, with a hairline at its left edge, like a footnote to the headline. No intermediate size exists.
Photo modes: the photo is a cropped band, not a card. Below: bleeds off the right and bottom edges, left edge on the 96px margin. On top: bleeds off the left edge, right edge on the right margin, and on a scene photo the headline's first line overlaps the band's bottom edge on a gradient scrim (a subject photo never gets text over it).
Type-only modes: the headline is the architecture (top or low anchor); the body hangs from the same axis as a counter-weight. The icon stays atmosphere behind.
Eye path (drawn for photo-below): Entry the orange tail of the headline, Read the body on the axis one step down-left, Settle on the photo band. Other modes keep headline then body; only the band and the anchor move.
Orange moment: the headline's hook span (data-driven). On the orange canvas leading opens up so the ink hook chip clears the line above.
Why more premium than title-over-photo: scale contrast and a visible axis give structure with almost no ornament, and the photo edge is built from the same grid as the type.
